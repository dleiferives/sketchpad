Use case: ui-mockup
Asset type: design exploration board for a native tablet drawing app called Sketchpad.
Primary request: create a beautifully resolved, high fidelity product design comparison showing THREE distinct ways to control a drawing app with a pen. This is a concept board, not a screenshot of a shipped app. Wide landscape board, three equally sized large landscape app mockups stacked vertically with generous editorial whitespace, thin separators and small captions. Each mockup shows the same restrained expressive graphite drawing of a ceramic vase and a leafy branch, on an expansive warm white canvas. No device bezels or hands. Precise native UI, extremely legible typography, warm ivory chrome, charcoal icons, one muted forest-green selected state, very subtle shadows, 44px-equivalent pen targets.
Mockup 1 title: "01  Tool rail". Narrow floating vertical tool rail, elegant pen and eraser nib symbols, a circular color chip, tiny size and opacity values; an expanded brush card off the rail shows sliders, labels "Size" "12 px", "Opacity" "80%", four small swatches. Compact top-left Sketchpad/undo/redo strip. Mostly canvas.
Mockup 2 title: "02  Brush puck". Much less permanent chrome: lower corner has one circular two-part puck with an ink nib above and a forest-green color chip below. Show a crisp large magnified callout beside the screenshot with horizontal arrows labelled "Size" and vertical arrows labelled "Opacity". Tiny peripheral undo, eraser, eyedropper controls; no large sidebar. A small floating readout next to the puck says "12 px · 80%".
Mockup 3 title: "03  Pen menu". Transient six-wedge marking menu at the pen cursor over canvas. Clearly labelled sectors "Pen", "Eraser", "Color", "Pick", "Undo", "Pan". Center is the current color; a small note below reads "Press, slide, release". Compact background tools recede.
Constraints: clearly distinguish all three interaction concepts, realistic shippable interface proportions, avoid feature-heavy toolbars, no neon, no gradients except color controls, no stock-photo styling, no invented marketing text, no gibberish labels. Treat artwork as subtle shared context so controls are the subject.
